DietApp Pro

Privacy Policy

Effective 4 November 2026

The short version

DietApp Pro doesn't collect your data. Your nutrition log, targets and body measurements stay on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud account. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and the app contains no third-party code that collects data.

What the app stores

The app stores your food log, calorie and macro targets, saved foods, meals and recipes, body measurements and settings on your device. The developer has no access to it.

iCloud sync

When you're signed in to iCloud, the app syncs this data through your private iCloud database (Apple's CloudKit) to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. It is stored in your iCloud account under Apple's privacy policy, not on servers the developer controls, and the developer can't access it. Weight and body fat read from Apple Health are never synced: they stay on each device, which reads them from Health itself. Settings › iCloud shows whether sync is on.

Settings that affect your day's numbers — training-day extras, the water and fasting goals, and meal order and visibility — also sync through your iCloud account's key-value storage, which only you and your devices can read.

You can delete your data at any time under Settings › Data › Delete All Data, which removes it from this device, your other synced devices and iCloud. Deleting the app removes it from that device only; data in iCloud stays until you delete it in the app or in your iCloud settings.

Online food databases

To look up nutritional information, the app uses two third-party food databases:

  • Open Food Facts (world.openfoodfacts.org), a community-run open database of food products. Open Food Facts privacy policy.
  • USDA FoodData Central (api.nal.usda.gov), the U.S. Department of Agriculture's food composition database. USDA privacy policy.

When you search for a food, scan a product barcode, or log a meal by description, the food text or barcode is sent to Open Food Facts first, with USDA FoodData Central as the fallback. These requests contain only the food names or barcodes you ask about, and the app's name and version. They never contain your food log, targets, body measurements, Health data or anything else stored in the app. Like any internet request, they reveal your device's IP address to the provider; neither provider receives an account, name or other identifier from the app.

You can turn each database off under Settings › Food Data Sources; the app then looks up foods only in its on-device catalog. Each provider's own privacy policy applies to its service.

Apple Health

Only if you turn it on in Settings › Apple Health, the app connects to Apple Health. You choose exactly which data it may read and write in the Health app.

  • It writes: the foods you log (dietary energy, protein, carbohydrates, fat and, where known, fiber, sugar, sodium, saturated fat, cholesterol, caffeine, vitamins and minerals), the water you log, and the weight and body fat you enter in the app.
  • It reads: weight and body fat recorded by other apps and devices, which are added to your measurements in the app; active energy, to add exercise calories to your daily target if you switch that on; and blood pressure, blood glucose and sleep, shown on the Progress tab.

Health data is only used to power the app's nutrition and progress tracking. It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, never shared with anyone, never sent to the developer, and never stored in iCloud by the app: weight and body fat read from Health stay on the device. You can turn this off at any time in Settings or in the Health app.

Apple Intelligence

Describing a meal in natural language uses Apple Intelligence on your device. Your description is understood on the device and is never sent to the developer. To find the nutrition of the foods it names, only those food names are looked up in the online food databases described above, unless you've turned them off. When the on-device model is unavailable, the app falls back to basic text parsing, also on the device.

Camera and photos

The camera is used to scan product barcodes and, when you choose to, to photograph a meal. You can also pick a meal photo from your photo library; the app only sees the photo you pick. Barcode images are processed on the device to read the barcode. Meal photos are analysed on the device by Apple Intelligence to suggest foods and portions, which you can edit before anything is saved. Images are never stored by the app and are never sent to the developer or to third parties.

Reminders, widgets and Siri

Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device. Widgets and Siri or Shortcuts actions read today's totals from the app on your device; nothing is sent to the developer.

Exports and backups

CSV and JSON exports and full backups are created on your device only when you choose to. Where you save or send them is up to you — for example iCloud Drive or another app — and that service handles them under its own privacy policy. A backup contains everything stored in the app, including weight and body fat imported from Apple Health, so keep it somewhere private.

Children

DietApp Pro isn't directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be in the app and on this page, with a new effective date.
